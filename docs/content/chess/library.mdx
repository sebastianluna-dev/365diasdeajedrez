# Chess library (`lib/chess`)

Every module here is **pure**: no React, no Prisma, no `server-only` (except where Node's `crypto` is needed). They are unit-tested (`*.test.ts`, fixtures in `lib/chess/__fixtures__/`) and shared by the browser components, the services, the seed and the scripts. The rules engine underneath is [`chessops`](https://github.com/niklasf/chessops); the board is [`@lichess-org/chessground`](https://github.com/lichess-org/chessground).

## Conventions

- **PGN in, positions out.** Nothing stores moves relationally; every consumer parses the PGN it needs.
- **Dotted paths** address a node inside a PGN tree: child indexes from the root, `"0"` is the first move, `"0.0.1.0"` goes down the mainline twice, into the second variation, then one more move. `children[0]` is the mainline by convention (which is what `makePgn` assumes when serialising). Stored in `TrainingExercise.path` and `ClassBlock.movePath`.
- **Paths move.** Deleting or promoting reindexes siblings; hold a node reference and recompute the path with `pathOfNode` after mutating.
- **Spanish SAN for display only.** `sanToSpanish` maps `N→C, B→A, R→T, Q→D, K→R` (and the promotion piece); storage and comparisons keep English SAN.
- **Evaluations are always from White's point of view**, whether they come from the engine or from `[%eval]` comments. A mate already on the board is not an evaluation: Stockfish reports it as `score mate 0`, a zero with no sign that cannot say who won, so `parseEngineInfo` returns `null` for it, and an `[%eval #0]` that a game reviewed before that change still carries is read by `parsePgnTree` for the side that gave the mate (only the side to move can be mated).

## Typical flows

**Import a PGN into a study**

1. `readImportedGames(text)` (chessops `parsePgn`; it also counts what it leaves out — entries without moves, games past `PGN_MAX_GAMES` — so the import can say it) → headers to columns, result token to catalog code, full dates only, Elo/titles/federations.
2. For each game, `indexGamePositions(tx, { gameId, databaseId, pgn })` → `extractGamePositions` → `GamePosition` rows; replay warnings are logged, not fatal.
3. All inside one `$transaction` with `PGN_IMPORT_TRANSACTION`.

**Mark a lesson trainable**

1. `extractMainline(lessonPgnOf(lesson))` → SANs + initial FEN (null if no moves → rejected).
2. Choose the student's colour (`trainingColorId` or the side to move).
3. `deriveExerciseData({ initialFen, afterSans, lineSans })` → frozen `startFen`, `line`, `path`, plies; stored as a derived `TrainingExercise`.

**Evaluate a game**

1. `replayGame(pgn)` → FEN per ply.
2. A dedicated Stockfish worker analyses each position at depth 16 (`use-game-review.hook.ts`), collecting `MoveEvaluation`s.
3. `reviewGame(positions)` → accuracy, grades, phases; `setEvaluation` can write them back into the PGN as `[%eval]`.

## Exercises: the format and the solver

An exercise's solution is a PGN: `[SetUp "1"]`, `[FEN]` (the start position) and the moves, the player's and the opponent's replies, with variants, comments and NAGs. The **player is the side to move** at the start, so every even depth of the tree (the first move, the third…) is the player's. Nothing here runs an engine (D03).

**Storing** (`readSolutionStart`). It does not trust `parsePgnTree`'s fallback, which reads a broken `[FEN]` as the standard start: `startingPosition` must accept the `[FEN]` (`badPosition` otherwise), `positionPlausibility` must not rule it out (`badPosition`), and a `[Variant]` other than none, `Standard` or `From Position` is `unsupportedVariant`. Then, for any readable PGN, a draft's included, the frozen columns are deterministic: `startFen` is chessops' normalised start (an impossible castling right or en passant square dropped) and the stored `[FEN]` is rewritten to it; the tree is copied with each move in chessops' canonical SAN (`Rd8` is stored `Rd8#`) and cut at the first illegal move of each branch, where `parsePgnTree` stops reading it; only `SetUp`/`FEN` stay among the headers; `solutionPlies` counts the legal main line and `playerMoves` = ⌈plies / 2⌉. Reading the stored `normalizedPgn` again gives the same columns.

**Readiness** (`analyzeSolution`, for READY, PUBLISHED and an import as ready). Problems block: `noMoves`; `illegalMove` (one per branch `parsePgnTree` cut); `mateInMissing` (a «Mate en N» without an N from 1 to `MATE_IN_MAX`, 5) and `mateInOnSequence`; `mistakeVariant` (an accepted player variant — a player move that is not its node's main child — marked `?`, `??` or `?!`, which D02 would accept as an answer; `dropMistakeVariants` deletes them, «Eliminar las variantes marcadas como error»). For «Mate en N»: every line of the tree, the opponent's defences included, must end with **the player's** move mating (`notMate`), none may take more than N player moves (`mateTooLong`), the main line takes exactly N (`mateLengthMismatch`), and for N ≤ `FORCED_MATE_SEARCH_MAX` (2) an exhaustive search by the rules checks that the key move mates in N against **every** defence, not only the written ones (`notForced`); for N ≥ 3 the author's lines are trusted. Warnings never block: `opponentVariant` (replies always follow the main line; the other defences are only shown), `playerAlternatives` (several accepted moves at one point, «Se aceptan: Ab8#, Da8#») and, in a sequence, `shortAlternative` (an accepted variant that ends before the main line).

**Shaping.** `emptySolutionPgn(fen)` is the editor's start; `exerciseSeedFromGame(pgn, path)` is «Crear ejercicio desde aquí» and «Desde una partida»: the position after the move at `path` and, as the solution, the whole subtree after it, copied node by node (the `variationPgn` rule), or null for a broken path; `solutionPgnFromLine(fen, sans)` is a frozen line as a solution (the seed, the phase 2 migration).

**Solving** (`exercise-solver.ts`):

1. **Matching** (D02). A move matches any child of the node, the main line or a variant, compared as moves (`parseSan` → `makeUci`), so `Rd8` is `Rd8#`, both e1→g1 and e1→h1 arrive as `O-O` (`sanForMove`), and `=N` is not `=Q`. A move outside the tree is wrong, even one that mates. A matched variant is followed from then on.
2. **The reply** (D01) is always the main child, `children[0]`.
3. **Completion**: a matched player move, or its reply, with no children.
4. **The first wrong move fails** (D19): it is recorded (`firstMistake`: the ply within the solution, the FEN, the played and the expected move in canonical SAN, whether a hint came before) and fixes the decision; the board takes it back and the student may go on, but nothing after the decision is the attempt's (`requiredMoves`, `correctMoves` and `reachedPly` are frozen).
5. **Hints** (D20, D37): every press counts; the author's texts in order, then the from-square of the main child's move, at any step. Over, there is no hint (`null`).
6. **«Ver la solución»** (D20): the main line from where the board stands, with its comments; the attempt is failed and over.
7. **Outcome** (D33): PASSED is completed and never failed; perfect is PASSED with no hint; the review hears `clean`, `hinted` or `failed`.
8. **Server replay** (`replayAttempt`): the player's moves (with the hints before each) replayed from the start must reach the decision they claim — all matched and completed, all matched but the last, or all matched and the solution shown. A move after the decision (`movesAfterDecision`), a move the board cannot play (`illegal`) or a sequence that decides nothing (`undecided`) is refused. The hints only lower the outcome, so they are taken as sent; the SANs and the FEN of the mistake are the server's.
