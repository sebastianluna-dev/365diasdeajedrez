# Scripts & seed

All scripts run with `tsx` outside Next, load `.env.local` then `.env`, and build their own `PrismaClient` with `@prisma/adapter-pg` (the app's singleton is `server-only`). They fail fast if `PLATFORM_DATABASE_URL` is missing.

## `prisma/seed.ts` — `npm run db:seed`

Idempotent seed of the platform database: catalogs by `upsert(code)` (the levels by `slug`, with `seedLevels`, because their rows carry a description and an Elo range), domain rows by fixed ids or natural keys, so running it twice never duplicates.

What it creates (data in `prisma/seed-data.ts`):

- every catalog value (`seedCatalog` for `[code, label]` pairs; `seedCatalogRows` for a catalog with more columns, like `ContentType` with its `shortLabel` and `description`) and the topic tree;
- eight demo accounts — the student, the teacher (with an active `Teacher` row), the staff, and the five classmates of the classes demo (`prisma/seed-classes.ts`: Valentina Cruz, Mateo Ríos, Diego Salas, Lucía Herrera, Andrea López, each with their default study; all but Lucía assigned to the demo teacher) — the password is only set when the account has none;
- an author, the courses with chapters, lessons (PGN or linked games), topics and the lessons' exercises — `Exercise` rows owned by the lesson since phase 2 of [Exercises](/platform/exercises#courses-phase-2) (2026-10-16): by fixed id, the start replayed with `deriveExerciseData`, the solution a PGN from `solutionPgnFromLine`, `exerciseSolutionColumns` spread as the writers do, PUBLISHED as seeded data, their topics through `syncCatalogJoin`; and the demo student's three attempts at them as `isLegacy`, as the migration left them; the role the author signs each course with (`SeedCourse.authorRole`, «Autor del contenido» by default) and its «Procedencia del recurso» (`SeedCourse.sources`);
- studies and games for the demo student (positions indexed with `indexGamePositions`), shared collections;
- the classes demo (`prisma/seed-classes.ts`, 2026-10-15): five payments and twenty classes around the reference day D — one in every phase at 11:40 of D (the Najdorf with its link open at noon, a class without a link, one without a topic, a group class of six seats in three days with one seat «Sin pago», one that ended two days ago and nobody finalized, one the staff cancelled, a free diagnosis, a no-show whose seat the staff moved to a new date, a mark the staff changed after a report), the blocks of three classes with their visibility and a transcript; the facts are written as data by fixed id, the derived columns through the writers' pure functions, and the seats through `syncClassSeat`/`moveClassSeat`, so re-seeding is idempotent and the payments' invariant holds ([Classes](/platform/classes)); `prisma/seed-classes.test.ts` checks the phases, the seats, the balances and the overlaps without a database;
- «Ejercicios» (`prisma/seed-exercises.ts`, 2026-10-16, phase 1 of [Exercises](/platform/exercises)): the catalogs `ExerciseKind` and `ExerciseStatus`, the attempt contexts and the 19 tactical topics under «Táctica» (`TOPIC_VALUES` carries each topic's order and parent); five `READY` exercises of the demo teacher — «El pasillo abierto» (mate in 1), «El mate de Philidor» (mate in 2), «Mate de las negras» (black mates in 1), «Horquilla de caballo» (a sequence with a defence variant) and «La enfilada en la columna» (two hints and an explanation) —; his series «Táctica de la semana» with the five, given to the demo student; and three attempts of the student in it (one clean, one failed at the first move with its mistake, one with a hint), so two of the five are still untried. The rows go by fixed id (`EXERCISE_IDS`), the solution spreads `exerciseSolutionColumns` as the writers do and the status is seeded data (the documented exception to the single writers); each attempt is stored with the outcome the server's replay gives its moves, the student's `UserExerciseState` is folded by `rebuildUserExerciseState`, and the two first passes' `EXERCISE_PASSED` activities go by fixed id before the daily stats are rebuilt. `prisma/seed-exercises.test.ts` runs `analyzeSolution` and the readiness check on every exercise and replays every attempt, without a database. A re-seed rewrites the same rows and dates the three attempts again from the reference instant; it rewrites the seeded series' items whole (an exercise the teacher added to «Táctica de la semana» from the app is dropped from it), and keeps any attempt the student made since, which the rebuild folds with the rest;
- activities and the rebuilt `UserStatDaily` aggregates;
- «Jugar» (`prisma/seed-play.ts`): its three catalogs (the bot group with its `isListed`) and the 57 bots — the 45 of `lib/chess/bots/roster.ts` and the 12 rivals of «El cuaderno del viajero» —, upserted by code and slug from `allBotSeeds()`; three games for the demo student — won, lost by resignation, and one open on their turn — only when they have played none, and nine in the adventure only when they have played none against its rivals, which leave the map at **4 of 12** (★★★, ★★, ★ and ★★★, and a lost first try at the fifth, «Intento 2»). A re-seed never touches a game played since, and a database that already held the first three gets the nine. They write no activity: playing never counts toward the streak.

Environment switches:

| Variable                        | Effect                                                                                                                           |
| ------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| `PLATFORM_DEMO_PASSWORD`        | Password for the demo accounts (default `ajedrez365`).                                                                           |
| `ALLOW_DEMO_SEED=1`             | Allows seeding a **non-local** database with the default password. Test databases only.                                          |
| `SEED_NOW=2026-09-01T18:00:00Z` | Reference instant; without it, today at 18:00 UTC. Pin it to reproduce a database byte for byte, or to anchor a demo on its day. |
| `NODE_ENV=production`           | Treated as remote: the guard applies.                                                                                            |

The guard: if the database host is not `localhost`/`127.0.0.1`/`::1` (or `NODE_ENV=production`) and the password is the repository default and `ALLOW_DEMO_SEED` is not `1`, the seed throws before writing anything.

**After a seed, run `npm run collections:by-chapter`.** The seed leaves every lesson with its own PGN and no game; this script files that PGN as a game of the chapter's collection and links the lesson to it. Until it runs, a newly seeded course has an empty «Partidas» tab in the staff panel and its lessons show no linked game, which is what happened to the Fischer course on 2026-09-28. The script is idempotent (a lesson already linked is skipped) and documents why it uses one short transaction per game.

### What a second seed finds

The seed is not transactional and is meant to be run again on a database the app has already written to. Two things follow.

**It replaces what it describes.** For every course in `COURSES`, the chapters, lessons and sources that are not in the data are deleted before the rest is written. A chapter added to a seeded course from the staff panel — an introduction, a closing — is gone after the next seed, and so is a lesson added to one of its chapters. A lesson's exercises and class blocks are not deleted: the seed writes its own and leaves the rest, and so it does with the bank's exercises, series and shares — except the items of its own series, which it rewrites whole. Courses created from the panel are never touched.

**It adopts the collections the app created.** Two kinds of `GameDatabase` are also created by the app under ids of its own, and each is unique by something that is not the id: a chapter's collection (`@@unique([chapterId])`, created on demand by the staff panel's `importChapterGames` or by `collections:by-chapter`) and a user's default study (`game_database_one_default_per_user`, created with the account by `createDefaultStudy`). Inserting the seed's row next to them would break the index, so the seed looks the existing row up by that key, writes onto it and translates its own id into the real one for the shares, the games and the position index that point at it. Until 2026-09-28 it did not, and a second seed stopped at the first of those rows with `P2002`. The decision is `resolveCollectionIds` in `prisma/seed-rules.ts`, a pure module the seed hands its queries to, and `prisma/seed-rules.test.ts` holds it: which key each seeded collection has, that an existing row is adopted, that a second run lands on the same rows, and that no two seeded collections share a key.

**It puts its rows back in their order.** Four lists the seed writes are unique by their parent and an order: a course's chapters (`Chapter_courseId_order_key`), a chapter's lessons (`Lesson_chapterId_order_key`), a lesson's exercises (`Exercise_lessonId_lessonOrder_key`, over `lessonOrder`; an archived one has none and is never in the way) and a class's blocks (`ClassBlock_classId_order_key`). The seed upserts each row by id with the order its data gives, so once the staff had reordered a seeded course from the panel, swapped two of its exercises or deleted a chapter of a draft (the rest move up), or a teacher had moved the blocks of a seeded class, the first upsert found its order held by a sibling and the seed stopped there with `P2002`. Since 2026-10-03, right before writing a parent's children, it moves the rows that are not at their seeded order through temporary orders of their own, in one short transaction per parent: the two passes the panel's `planFullReorder` makes, and both go through `planMoves` in `services/shared/reorder.ts`. A row the seed does not own keeps its order unless the seed needs it; when one is in the way, it and the rows the seed does not own after it go right after the seeded ones, in the order they had. In practice that is an exercise made by hand or a class block a teacher added, since a chapter or a lesson of a seeded course that is not in the data has been deleted by then. A row at an order the seed never uses — the line trainer's derived drill at 0, in its own table since phase 2 — is below every seeded one and never moves. A second seed finds everything in place and moves nothing. The decision is `planSeededOrders` in `prisma/seed-rules.ts`, and `prisma/seed-rules.test.ts` replays its moves and the seed's upserts against the index: a course whose chapters the panel reordered, every seeded list reversed, a deleted chapter brought back, a row the seed does not know in the way and out of it, the rows the seed does not own keeping their order among themselves, the derived exercise left alone, a second seed with nothing to move, and data that gives two rows of one parent the same order refused before writing.

### Before a demo

Every date the seed writes hangs from one instant, `seedReferenceDate` in `prisma/seed-rules.ts`: today at 18:00 UTC, so two runs on the same day write the same rows, or `SEED_NOW`. The classes are placed on the reference day **D**, the date of that instant in Mexico City, at the academy's wall-clock hours; **D comes from the UTC day**, so a seed run between 18:00 and 24:00 in Mexico City gets D = tomorrow — to check the demo, seed with an explicit `SEED_NOW` (`SEED_NOW=2026-10-15T18:00:00Z npm run db:seed` puts the Najdorf at noon of the 15th). That is what a live demo wants (activity over the last days, a class a week ago and one three days ahead), and it is a snapshot: weeks later every class is behind and the dashboard's «Próxima clase» is empty. No seeded date can stay ahead of a clock that keeps moving, so the seed says when its demo runs out: its last line names the day of the next class («La próxima clase de la demo es el …»), or says there is none.

Before showing the platform, run `npm run db:seed` again: it is idempotent, adopts what the app created and puts back the order of what it describes (see above), so on a database nobody reordered it only moves the dates. To prepare a database days ahead, seed it with `SEED_NOW` set to the day of the demo; until that day the seeded activity sits in the future. `prisma/seed-rules.test.ts` holds the instant (same day, same instant; next day, one day later; `SEED_NOW` as given, and refused when it is not a date) and the next class.

### Courses that come from a book

A course whose content is somebody else's book lives in its own module under `prisma/seed-courses/`, with its data file in `prisma/seed-courses/data/`. The module does not write the content: it validates the file and translates it into a `SeedCourse`. To change the content the file is replaced.

| Module                        | Data file                                      | What it is                                                                                                                                                                                                                                            |
| ----------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| `think-like-a-grandmaster.ts` | `think-like-a-grandmaster.import.json`         | Kotov's _Piense como un gran maestro_: 162 diagrams over the book's 5 chapters, one position per lesson, plus a collection of 13 illustrative games.                                                                                                  |
| `my-60-memorable-games.ts`    | `my-60-memorable-games.pgn` (a Lichess export) | Fischer's _Mis 60 memorables partidas_: one whole annotated game per lesson, in three chapters by stage (1957–1959, 1960–1962, 1963–1967). No collection of its own in the seed: `collections:by-chapter` files each lesson as a game of its chapter. |

`my-60-memorable-games.ts` in detail:

- **The file is the export of the study** `https://lichess.org/study/Wq1xlajv`, verbatim. It carries **58 of the book's 60 games** — 59 and 60 are missing — and three reference games that the notes to game 55 cite. The course says so: the reference games are named «Partida de referencia N» and are not counted among the sixty.
- **`GAMES` is a correction table**, one entry per chapter of the study in its order, keyed by the study's `ChapterName`. The study is an amateur transcription: it misspells several players and attributes game 48 to Donald Byrne when it is Robert's. The lesson's title is rebuilt from the table and never read off the study's; every entry that departs from the study says why in `correction`. **The moves, the commentary and the study's own headers are not touched**, typos included.
- **The game's record is written from the table** (`recordOf`). The study exports no `White`, no `Black` and no result, its `Date` is the day the chapter was uploaded and its `Event` is the chapter's title; the platform reads a game's players, result and date off those headers when it files the lesson as a game, so as exported every game would be «Desconocido – Desconocido», still in play, in 2024. The five headers are rewritten: the players as corrected; `Date` as `1957.??.??`, which the importer reads as no date (only game 37 has a day, the study's); `Event` as `?` except for the six games a source names the tournament of; and `Result`, with the mark that closes the moves saying the same.
- **Where the results come from.** Fischer's closing note states it in all but four games. Of those, 39 is a draw by the record of the book the course was given; 19, 26 and 42 end on a winning position without the word and are wins by count: the book has 9 draws and 3 losses, and the nine and the three in `DRAWS` and `LOSSES` are all of them. The test holds the tally of the 58: 46 wins, 9 draws, 3 losses — the book's 48 less games 59 and 60.
- **The seed refuses a file that does not match the table** (count, order, `ChapterName`, a repeated or out-of-range game number), so replacing the export cannot silently reorder the games or bring a title nobody reviewed.
- **Lesson ids** are `f10000004g<number>` for the book's games and `f10000004r<n>` for the reference ones: a game keeps its id whatever its place in the chapter.
- The board is set from **Fischer's side**, the five games the book is known for (17, 18, 39, 48, 58) are `isPriority`, and the academy signs the course as «Adaptación digital».
- **Three sources**, because they are three things: the original work (_My 60 Memorable Games_, Simon & Schuster, 1969), the Spanish edition the commentary is translated in (La Casa del Ajedrez, ISBN 9788412510034) and the study. The Spanish edition has **no year**: Casa del Libro gives 2023 and Open Library 2022 for the same ISBN, so `publicationYear` is null and the disagreement is written in the notes.
- `my-60-memorable-games.test.ts` holds all of the above, and that every one of the 61 games is read whole by `parsePgnTree` with no warning, which is what an illegal move in the transcription would raise.

The commentary of both courses is third-party text still in copyright: fine for the academy's internal use, not for publishing without a licence. `WARNINGS.md` has the entry.

## `scripts/platform-user.ts` — `npm run user:*`

```bash
npm run user:create   -- alumno@correo.com "Nombre Apellido"   # asks for the password without echo
npm run user:password -- alumno@correo.com                      # new password + closes all sessions
npm run user:list                                               # accounts, has-password, open sessions
```

Passwords are never accepted as arguments (they would end up in the shell history); use `PLATFORM_USER_PASSWORD` for non-interactive runs. Validation uses the same `passwordProblem()` and `hashPassword()` as the app, and `user:create` enrols the student the way the staff panel does: the account and its "Mis partidas" study go in one transaction (`createDefaultStudy`).

## `scripts/index-game-positions.ts` — `npm run positions:index`

Fills `GamePosition` for games saved before the index existed, in batches of 200. `-- --all` deletes and regenerates every game's index; run it after changing FEN normalisation or the hash algorithm.

## `scripts/migrate-chapter-collections.ts` — `npm run collections:by-chapter`

One-off data migration from course-level collections to **chapter** collections (the SQL migration only added the column). Creates the chapter collections, moves games to the chapter of the lesson that references them (or the first chapter), and registers each lesson's own PGN as a game linked to it. Idempotent, resumable, `--dry` mode. Uses one short transaction per game because long interactive transactions with the pg adapter were observed to return `null` from `create` without throwing.

## `scripts/rebuild-daily-stats.ts` — `npm run stats:rebuild`

Rebuilds `UserStatDaily` from `UserActivity` through `services/shared/daily-stats.ts` (the same function the seed uses), for every account or for one (`-- <email>`). The aggregate is derived, so this is the answer whenever the bucketing changes — on 2026-09-21 the day moved from UTC to the study day, `America/Mexico_City` — or the two tables are suspected to have drifted apart. Each account is rebuilt in one transaction.

## `scripts/db-target.ts` — `npm run db:target`

Prints which databases `.env.local` points at — host, database name, and whether it is the local container or a managed one — for `PLATFORM_DATABASE_URL` and `DATABASE_URI`. Never the URL, which carries the password, so its output can be pasted and logged. It is the answer to "where am I about to write?" before a migration, a seed or `npm run dev`, all of which follow that file. The reading is `lib/database-target.ts`, a pure module the seed's guard uses too.

## `scripts/db-backup.ts` — `npm run db:backup`

Dumps those two databases (custom format) into `~/Backups/365diasdeajedrez/`, or into `-- --dir <folder>`: see [Deployment & backups](/operations/deployment#backups). It needs the container running (`npm run db:up`), because the `pg_dump` it uses is the container's. It writes nothing to any database.

## `scripts/make-board-sounds.ts` — `npm run sounds:make`

Not what the viewer plays today (that is lichess's `.mp3`, see [Stockfish, pieces & sounds](/chess/engine-and-assets)); this is the way out if they ever have to go. Writes `public/sounds/move.wav` and `capture.wav` by modal synthesis: a seeded noise burst through a two-pole low-pass (the strike) rings a bank of two-pole resonators (the wood), each with its frequency, level and decay in the script's `SPECS`. No third-party audio in the repository. Change the specs and run it again; the output is byte-for-byte reproducible. The values were tuned against measurements of lichess' standard recordings, never their samples. To switch: point `SOURCES` in `components/chess/game-viewer/move-sound.ts` at the `.wav` files.

## `scripts/sync-pieces.ts` — `npm run pieces:sync`

Regenerates `public/pieces/*.svg` from chessground's cburnett CSS (adds `viewBox`). Run after upgrading `@lichess-org/chessground`.

## `scripts/calibrate-bots.ts` — `npm run bots:calibrate`

Measures the bots of «Jugar» by playing them, and writes `lib/chess/bots/calibration.json`. It reads no `.env.local` and touches no database: it runs the engine the browser loads (`node public/engine/stockfish.js`) as child processes, two per worker. Always run it through `npm run` (the database hook does not ask for it then).

```bash
npm run bots:calibrate -- --bots tere,sofia,carlsen --games 120 --workers 6 --seed 7
npm run bots:calibrate -- --check            # measured, missing, relabelled, engine changed
npm run bots:calibrate -- --verify-openings  # the twelve openings are still balanced
```

Without `--bots` it measures all 45. The method, the anchors and how to read the figure are in [Bots](/chess/bots#calibration). Re-run it after any change to the strength table (`lib/chess/bots/bot-strength.ts`) or to the engine; `--check` says when the report has gone stale.

## `scripts/bots-sql.ts` — `npm run bots:sql`

Prints the SQL of «Jugar»'s catalogs (`BotGroup` with its `isListed`, `TimeControl`, `GameTermination`) and its 57 bots — the gallery's 45 and the 12 rivals of the adventure —, generated by `prisma/play-catalog-sql.ts` from `allBotSeeds()` (`lib/adventure/adventure-bots.ts`) and `constants/platform/play-codes.const.ts`. Production never runs the seed, so these rows reach it inside a migration: whenever the roster changes — a bot relabelled by the calibration, one switched off —, paste what this prints into a **new** migration. Every insert is an upsert by code or slug that never touches an `id` nor a bot's group. `prisma/play-catalog-sql.test.ts` fails until the latest migration that carries the block says what this prints. It touches no database.

## `scripts/adventure-art.ts` — `npm run adventure:art`

Exports the art of an adventure from its mockup. It reads no `.env.local` and touches no database: it opens the mockup (`planning/jugar/proposals/aventura/propuesta-b-v2.html`, local and ignored by git) in Chromium through Playwright, runs the mockup's own drawing code in the page (`scripts/lib/adventure-art-page.js`) and writes what it draws.

```bash
npm run adventure:art -- planning/jugar/proposals/aventura/propuesta-b-v2.html cuaderno-del-viajero
```

- **Scenery**: `public/aventuras/<slug>/{escritorio,tableta,movil}.svg` — each layout with the viewBox of the mockup's bleed (the paper runs 700 units past each side) — and `miniatura-alta.svg` and `miniatura-ancha.svg`, the cover's two miniatures. They carry NO live part: only the paper tape of the path is in them; the seam, the needle, the stamps, the pages of the notebook, the keepsakes, the pencil notes and the token are drawn by the app from data (`lib/adventure/adventures/<slug>.art.ts`). Unused `<defs>` are pruned, and the script warns when a file passes ~400 KB (then it is worth a pass of `svgo`, installed with `npx npm@10.8.2 install -D svgo` and a preset that keeps the ids the `<use>` elements point at). An SVG shown as an image cannot load fonts, so the few signs written in the scenery use the monospace fallback.
- **Source files**, formatted with Prettier's API so `npm run format:check` passes after a run: the twelve portraits (`components/adventure/portraits/<rival>-portrait.comp.tsx`, their ids made with `useId`) and a registry by rival slug; the keepsakes' drawings (`components/adventure/keepsakes/<slug>-keepsakes.const.tsx`) and the sealed envelope; and the pieces of the live layer the mockup draws, the token, the needle and the marks printed on the tape (`components/adventure/map/`).

The artwork is original to this project; edit the mockup and run the script again instead of editing a generated file. A new adventure is its mockup, this script, its seed and art files and a line in `lib/adventure/adventures.ts`.

## Adding a script

- Put it in `scripts/`, add an `npm run` alias, start with the `dotenv` lines and the connection guard.
- Share logic with the app through pure modules or modules that avoid `server-only` (`password.ts`, `logger.ts`, `position-hash.ts`, the catalog constants, `game-positions.service.ts` and `daily-stats.ts`, which take the client as a parameter).
- Prefer idempotent, resumable behaviour and a `--dry` flag for anything that writes.
