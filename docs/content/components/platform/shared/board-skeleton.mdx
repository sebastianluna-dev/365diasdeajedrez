## Purpose

The platform has two waits. **Arriving** at a screen shows the knight ([`BoardLoading`](/components/platform/shared/board-loading)): nothing of the screen is there yet. **Moving inside** a screen whose frame stays — from one lesson of a chapter to the next, from one game of a study to another — is a different wait: the column beside the board does not move, and a screen of another shape in the board's place would make the eye find the board again. There the board's place shows this: the board itself, without pieces, with a ring turning over it.

It was drawn inside `LessonSkeleton` until 2026-10-05, when a study's games got the same wait and the drawing became a piece of its own.

## Props

| Prop    | Type     | Required | Meaning                                                                                        |
| ------- | -------- | -------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| `label` | `string` | yes      | What is on its way, as a screen reader hears it: «Cargando la lección», «Cargando la partida». |

## Behaviour

- **The board is painted, not loaded.** A `conic-gradient` at `25% 25%` gives the eight by eight of the same two greens chessground paints (`--board-light` / `--board-dark`), so what arrives is the pieces and not a new board.
- **The box and what is painted in it are two different sizes, because they are in the viewer too.** The box is the whole width it is given and square, like the viewer's `.chess-board`; what is painted (its `::before`) is the side **rounded down to a whole 8** — `round(down, 100%, 8px)` —, which is the width of the viewer's board: its card is rounded the same way (`.game-viewer__board-card`), so that chessground, which rounds its board to whole device pixels per square, has nothing left to round. Painted edge to edge, the skeleton handed over a board up to 8px smaller and the swap blinked. A skeleton that draws strips around the board rounds its own track too, as [`GameSkeleton`](/components/platform/sections/studies/game-view/game-skeleton) does, and then the box and what is painted are the same.
- **One ring turning** (`@keyframes board-skeleton-turn`, 900ms) in the accent's soft shade — the house waiting, not an error — centred on the painted board: placed at half the rounded side and pulled back by half its own size with `translate: -50% -50%`, since `transform` is the rotation's. Under `prefers-reduced-motion` the ring stops and stays.
- `role="status"` with the `label` as its name: a screen reader is told what is on its way, once. The skeleton around it carries `aria-busy`.

## Markup & styling

Block `.board-skeleton` (63 lines): the box (`width: 100%`, `aspect-ratio: 1`), its painted `::before` and the ring.

**Elements:** `.board-skeleton__spinner`

**Keyframes:** `board-skeleton-turn`, at the top level because `@keyframes` cannot be nested.

**Tokens:** `--platform-accent`, `--platform-accent-soft`; the board's own `--board-light` and `--board-dark`.

No width media queries; `(prefers-reduced-motion: reduce)` stops the ring.

## Usage

From `components/platform/sections/studies/game-view/game-skeleton.comp.tsx`:

```tsx
import { BoardSkeleton } from "@/components/platform/shared/board-skeleton/board-skeleton.comp";

<BoardSkeleton label="Cargando la partida" />;
```

The skeleton that mounts it puts it in the board's own track, with the viewer's own numbers, so the two boards are the same box.
