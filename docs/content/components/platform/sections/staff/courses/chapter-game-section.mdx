## Purpose

Until 2026-09-28 the staff could paste a PGN into a chapter and remove a game, but not look at one nor correct it: a typo in a comment meant pasting the whole game again. This is the screen where a game of a course is read and edited, and it is the student's studies screen on purpose: the same [`GameViewer`](/components/chess/game-viewer/game-viewer) set the same way, the same autosave, the same undo, the same «Datos de la partida» dialog. Whoever knows one knows the other.

What is different is what the game IS. A student's game is a private analysis. A game of a course is the content of every lesson that uses it, so the aside says which lessons those are and the save looks after them.

## Props

### `ChapterGameSection`

| Prop        | Type                | Required | Meaning                                                                       |
| ----------- | ------------------- | -------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| `game`      | `ChapterGameDetail` | yes      | The game with its record, its PGN, its lessons and the chapter's other games. |
| `results`   | `CatalogOption[]`   | yes      | The results catalog, for the record's form; from `listGameResults()`.         |
| `errorCode` | `string`            | no       | `?error=` from saving the record, translated with `STAFF_ERROR_MESSAGES`.     |

`ChapterGameAside` takes `game` and `results`; `ChapterGameBoard` takes `game`. The aside hands [`GameList`](/components/platform/shared/game-list) the chapter's games (each `ChapterGameSibling` as a `GameListItem`: its label as the name, its players) and `reorderChapterGames` bound to the course and the chapter.

## Behaviour

- **Layout.** Two columns, the aside (280 px) and the board; under 1280 px the aside moves on top and its cards share the width. It is a workspace, without a big title: the board gets the height. There is no trail above the page, so the aside's first card says whose games these are — the chapter, its number and its course — and «Ver la colección» leads back.
- **The board** (`ChapterGameBoard`) is the one piece with state. Moves are played on it to add them; the move list's right click comments, annotates, promotes or deletes; Ctrl+Z / ⌘Z undoes. Every change is saved by itself 1.2 s after the last one, and the tools panel says how it went («guardando…», «guardado», «no se pudo guardar»). It is set from the side its first lesson shows the student (`game.orientation`).
- **It is keyed by the game**: jumping to another one from the list remounts the board, so its history and its autosave start clean instead of carrying the previous game's PGN over.
- **The record** is the `EditGame` dialog with `updateChapterGameDetails` bound to the game. `values={game}` whole: `ChapterGameDetail` carries every field of `GameFieldValues` under the same names.
- **«Lecciones que la usan»** links each lesson's editor, with the warning that what is changed here is what their students read.
- **The list** is the shared [`GameList`](/components/platform/shared/game-list), the same one a student's study shows: five rows tall with a scroll of its own, the current game brought into its middle on arrival. **It is also where the collection is put in order** (2026-09-30): each row is dragged by a grip that stays out of the way until the list is reached — what one comes here for is to jump between games —, and the keyboard moves a row from the grip with space and the arrows. The order is applied at once and saved in a transition (`useSortableRows` + `reorderChapterGames`); until 2026-09-30, a collection's order could only be decided by importing it again.
- **When a save is refused**, the reasons the staff can do something about get a notice above the board: `training` (a trainable lesson would be left without a line to train), `throttled`, `denied`.

### What saving does to the lessons

`autosaveChapterGamePgn` compares the moves of what is stored with the moves of what arrives (`sameMoves`, in `services/shared/game-record`): the same starting position and the same tree, move by move. Comments, glyphs, arrows and headers do not count.

| What changed                 | The game                       | Its lessons                                                                                                                                                |
| ---------------------------- | ------------------------------ | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| A comment, a glyph, an arrow | PGN saved, positions reindexed | Nothing. Their exercises stay as they were.                                                                                                                |
| A move, anywhere in the tree | PGN saved, positions reindexed | `pgnUpdatedAt` is stamped, which marks as stale the exercises frozen against the previous moves, and the derived exercise of a trainable lesson is remade. |
| The record (the dialog)      | Columns and PGN headers saved  | Nothing: the moves are not touched.                                                                                                                        |

All of it in one transaction. If a trainable lesson were left without a line to train — the main line emptied, or only the opponent's move left — nothing is saved and the answer is `training`: the same halfway state `updateLesson` refuses.

The three ids of the URL are part of every query (`database: { chapterId, courseId }`), in the service and in both actions: a game of another chapter, of another course or of a student's study answers like one that does not exist.

### A re-seed does not undo an edit

The seed writes the lessons' own `pgn`; a lesson linked to a game shows the GAME's PGN (`lessonPgnOf`), and the seed does not touch the games `collections:by-chapter` created. So what the staff corrects here survives `npm run db:seed`. The other side of that: replacing a course's data file does not reach the lessons already filed as games.

## Markup & styling

Three blocks, one per file; the list is `GameList`'s own block, `.game-list`.

- `.chapter-game`: element `.chapter-game__layout`. Breakpoint `(width < 1280px)`, one column.
- `.chapter-game-aside`: elements `__card`, `__head`, `__head-row`, `__chapter`, `__count`, `__course`, `__back`, `__record-head`, `__label`, `__event`, `__site`, `__meta`, `__meta-cell`, `__meta-key`, `__meta-value`, `__foot`, `__lessons`, `__lesson`, `__note`; modifier `__card_variant_lessons`. Breakpoint `(width < 1280px)`, the cards side by side.

**Tokens:** `--platform-accent-hover`, `--platform-accent-ink`, `--platform-border`, `--platform-surface`, `--platform-text-subtle`

## Usage

From `app/(platform)/administracion/cursos/[courseId]/capitulos/[chapterId]/partidas/[gameId]/page.tsx`:

```tsx
import { ChapterGameSection } from "@/components/platform/sections/staff/courses/chapter-game/chapter-game.section";

const game = await getChapterGame(courseId, chapterId, gameId);
if (!game) notFound();
const results = await listGameResults();

<ChapterGameSection game={game} results={results} errorCode={error} />;
```
