## Purpose

Who each course is aimed at: «Iniciación», «Principiante», «Intermedio», «Avanzado», «Experto», «Maestro», each with an orientative, inclusive Elo range. Unlike the catalogs the forms only read, this one is **edited from the interface**, so its rows are identified by a `slug` and not by a `code` the logic compares. Like [authors](/components/platform/sections/staff/courses/authors-manager) and [entities](/components/platform/sections/staff/entities/entities-admin-section), nothing is deleted: a level is retired, and the courses that had it keep their assignment.

The ranges are an internal classification, not official categories nor equivalences between FIDE, Chess.com and Lichess; «Maestro» names how hard the content is, not a title. The section says so under the table (`LEVEL_CATALOG_NOTE`), next to the sentence the student reads wherever a range appears (`ELO_DISCLAIMER`, «El Elo es orientativo»). Nothing in the platform restricts a course by Elo.

| `errorCode` | `string`          | no       | `?error=` translated with `STAFF_ERROR_MESSAGES` (`levelElo`, `levelSlug`, `levelSlugShape`, `levelMissing`, `invalid`, `throttled`). |

## Behaviour

- **Summary table** «Rangos orientativos»: level, range as the student reads it (`eloRange`: "Menos de 800", "800–1199", "2200 o más"), description and how many courses have it. It is where one checks that the ranges meet end to end; a retired level is greyed and tagged «Retirado».
- **One card per level** with its own [`LevelForm`](/components/platform/sections/staff/levels/level-form), so saving one never touches another.
- **«Nuevo nivel»** at the end, which is where a new level lands when it is not told its position.
- The ranges are not forced to be contiguous nor disjoint: they are orientative, a course can have several levels, and moving a boundary takes two saves.
