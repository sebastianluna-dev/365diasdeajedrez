## Purpose

The notation as a table, the way Lichess shows a game: one row per move number with White's and Black's moves as cells, and everything that is not the main line (comments, variations at any depth) as full-width blocks between the rows. This is the list for **consulting** a game, which is what the studies do; lessons use the [`MoveTree`](/components/chess/game-viewer/move-tree).

The hard part, deciding the order of rows and blocks and when Black's move has to be pushed to a new row after a variation, is not here: `lib/chess/notation-blocks.ts` is pure and tested against a real game with three levels of variations. This component only renders the blocks it returns.

## Behaviour

- `buildNotationBlocks(tree)` is memoised and yields three block kinds: `row` (number, white, black, with `continuation` and `pushedBlack` flags rendered as "…" cells), `comment` (a paragraph) and `line` (an inline variation with its `depth`, exposed as the `--move-table-depth` custom property for indentation).
- Each move cell shows the Spanish SAN, its NAG glyphs (hidden from the screen reader, which skips "!" and "?" and has no name for "⩲", and said to it in words by a visually hidden `.move-table__sr`: "Db8+, Jugada brillante", through `nagLabel`) and, when the node carries an evaluation, `formatEvaluation` of it. The evaluation comes from the PGN's `[%eval]`, so a reviewed game shows its numbers again on reload with no engine involved.
- **The size is Lichess's on a desktop**: moves in the scale's `sm` (15px; 16 from 1536px) at a line height of 1.4, in rows of 29px — they were 17px in rows of 34 until 2026-10-04 —, so a panel as tall as the board shows about a sixth more of the game. Comments and variations are a step under the moves (`xs`), and an evaluation two (`2xs`). Full screen sets its own, larger size through `--move-tree-font-size`.
- The active move is kept at the **middle** of the list on every path change, as Lichess keeps it (`scrollToMiddle` from `components/chess/scroll-within.ts`, 2026-10-03): going through a game, the moves just played and the ones to come are both in sight, where before the move rode the list's bottom edge (it was only brought into view by the least it took). At either end of the game the scroll stops where the list ends, and back at the starting position, which has no move, the list returns to its top. The scroll is instant — holding an arrow walks a move every 130 ms — and only the list's own scroller moves: `scrollIntoView` would also move the page, or the canvas the table is drawn on.
- **The owner's pinned comment heads the list** (2026-10-05): a `.move-table__comment` like any other, first of all, and then the PGN's opening comment, since the pinned one is about the game in the study and the PGN's is something the game says. It keeps the line breaks it was typed with (`_kind_pinned`, `white-space: pre-line`), which a PGN comment does not, because a PGN may be wrapped at any column.
- `bind(node)` attaches the ref, click and optional right-click handlers to both row cells and inline variation moves, so the two look different but behave the same.

## Markup & styling

Block `.move-table` (298 lines). The evaluations are in `--platform-text-muted`, not a lighter grey: the cell turns to `--platform-surface-neutral` under the pointer, a fill where the subtle grey falls under 4.5:1. On the active move — in the material look too, on the primary's blue, where the grey read under 2:1 until 2026-10-23 — they take its accent and stay behind it by their weight (500 against the move's 700), not by an opacity.

**Modifiers:** `.move-table_look_material`.

**Elements:** `.move-table__row`, `.move-table__number`, `.move-table__cell`, `.move-table__move`, `.move-table__nag`, `.move-table__sr` (the NAGs in words, visually hidden), `.move-table__eval`, `.move-table__ellipsis`, `.move-table__comment`, `.move-table__line`, `.move-table__inline`, `.move-table__inline-comment`

**The block is `position: relative`** (2026-10-06): it is the containing block of `.move-table__sr`, the words read and not seen, which are `position: absolute`. Without it they were placed against the body, escaped the panel's own scroll and stretched the page by the length of the game — which is what gave the full-window lesson a scrollbar.

**Modifiers and states:** `.move-table__comment_kind_pinned` (the owner's note: keeps its line breaks), `.move-table__cell_side_white`, `.move-table__cell_side_black`, `.move-table__move_state_active`, `.move-table__inline_state_active`

**Tokens:** own `--move-table-depth` (set inline per variation) and `--move-table-step` (indent per depth); `--move-tree-font-size` shared with the tree; `--platform-accent-ink`, `--platform-accent-soft`, `--platform-bg`, `--platform-border`, `--platform-border-strong`, `--platform-surface-neutral`, `--platform-text`, `--platform-text-muted`, `--platform-text-subtle` (the evaluations, and the «…» of a move shown on another row)

No media queries.

## Usage

From `components/chess/game-viewer/game-viewer.comp.tsx`:

```tsx
import { MoveTable } from "./move-table.comp";

<MoveTable
  tree={tree}
  currentPath={currentPath}
  onSelect={setCurrentPath}
  onContextMenu={isEditing ? openMenu : undefined}
  pinnedComment={pinnedComment}
/>;
```
