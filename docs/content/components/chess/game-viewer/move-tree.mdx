## Purpose

The notation as prose. Moves run on one after another and a **comment breaks the line**: the run closes carrying the move the comment explains, the comment follows as its own paragraph, and the line resumes below with its number repeated ("3… Ab4"). That is how a lesson is read — a paragraph of moves, then what is said about the last of them, with the board alongside.

A variation breaks the line too, into a paragraph of its own. The **first level carries no parentheses**: what says it is another line is its colour (`--platform-variation`, a grey: what was played is the black of the page and what was only proposed steps back from it), and what says where it ends is the main line starting again below it with its number repeated. Its own comment goes on in that same paragraph, in the page's ink — it is what the line is being proposed for, and read in the variation's grey it would look like more notation. The move of a variation that is pointed at or looked at is backed by `--platform-variation-soft` and written in `--platform-text-muted`, a step darker: on that backing the variation's own grey falls under 4.5:1.

A variation **of** a variation does carry them, because there the colour no longer tells one line from the other and only the parentheses say which line one has come back to. There is **no indentation** at any level: a line pushed further in at every step ends up read as a note in the margin rather than as chess.

This is the list a **lesson** uses; a game consulted move by move uses the [`MoveTable`](/components/chess/game-viewer/move-table).

The tree carries no editing logic. It reports a right click when asked to and nothing else, so the student's viewer never ships menu code.

## Behaviour

- Where the line is cut is not decided here: `buildProseBlocks` (`lib/chess/notation-prose`) returns the runs and comments in reading order, already labelled in Spanish piece letters, with each move's NAGs apart (`nags`): the button draws their glyphs hidden from the screen reader, which skips "!" and "?" and has no name for "⩲", and says them in words in a visually hidden `.move-tree__sr` (`nagLabel`): "16. Db8+, Jugada brillante". It is a pure module with tests, because the subtle part — when a move has to repeat its number — is checked far better that way than by looking at a screen.
- The component walks that list and draws it: a run becomes a `<p>` of buttons, a comment a `<p>` of text, and a variation a `<p>` of its own. The brackets are hung on the blocks (`opens`, `closes`) and not written into the text, because a variation with one of its own is cut into several paragraphs: the `(` goes on the first and the `)` on whichever paragraph its last move fell in — which may belong to a deeper variation ending there too, so `closes` is a count and not a flag.
- The active move is scrolled to the **top** of the panel on every path change: what is read after a move is what comes after it, and kept merely in view the move being followed crept down the panel until everything worth reading was below the fold. At the end of the game it stops where the scroll ends, as any scroll does.
- That scroll is done **by hand**, not with `scrollIntoView`. That method scrolls every scrollable ancestor, and an ancestor with `overflow: hidden` still counts — it cannot be scrolled by hand but it can by script — so on the lesson, which is a page that fills the window and hides what overflows, it pushed the board and the panel up out of the screen. The component looks for its own scroller (`scrollerOf` from `components/chess/scroll-within.ts`: the nearest ancestor with `overflow: auto | scroll` on either axis, since who provides it changes) and moves only that one, converting the distance from the page's px to the scroller's with `currentCSSZoom` (1 since the platform lost its zoom).
- With `onContextMenu`, the button's `onContextMenu` prevents the browser menu and forwards the path and the event (the viewer needs the pointer position to place its menu).

## Markup & styling

Block `.move-tree` (120 lines).

**Elements:** `.move-tree__line` (a run of the main line), `.move-tree__comment` (a paragraph of the lesson), `.move-tree__variation` (a line that was not played), `.move-tree__bracket`, `.move-tree__aside` (what a variation is proposed for, inside its paragraph), `.move-tree__move`, `.move-tree__sr` (a move's NAGs in words, visually hidden)

**Modifiers and states:** `.move-tree__comment_kind_pinned` (the owner's note heading the text: keeps its line breaks), `.move-tree__move_active`

**Tokens:** `--move-tree-font-size` (set by the viewer's panel so the list scales with it), `--platform-accent-ink`, `--platform-accent-soft`, `--platform-surface-neutral`, `--platform-text`, `--platform-text-muted`, `--platform-variation`, `--platform-variation-soft`

No media queries; it wraps as text.

## Usage

From `components/chess/game-viewer/game-viewer.comp.tsx`:

```tsx
import { MoveTree } from "./move-tree.comp";

<MoveTree
  tree={tree}
  currentPath={currentPath}
  onSelect={setCurrentPath}
  onContextMenu={isEditing ? openMenu : undefined}
  pinnedComment={pinnedComment}
/>;
```
