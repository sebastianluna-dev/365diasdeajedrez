# Storybook

Storybook renders a component in isolation, with its real stylesheet, fonts and tokens, and lets you flip its states from a controls panel. It complements the [Components](/components) reference: that section says what a component receives and does; Storybook shows it.

**Status:** adopted for the platform's shared pieces: every piece of `components/platform/shared/` has a story, and `components/stories.test.ts` holds it, with the rules below (`AGENTS.md`, «Componentes»). Elsewhere a story is still optional. The stories are the `components/**/*.stories.tsx` files, and that glob is the list: the platform's [foundations](#foundations) (its colours, the text each fill takes, corners, motion and type, drawn from the stylesheets) and its [primitives](#primitives) (the global classes of `platform.css`: button, row action, card, tag, dialog, page), the site's `PlanCard`, where the pilot began, the [icon gallery](#overview) (`components/icons/gallery.stories.tsx`, a page like the foundations), every chess piece of `components/chess/` but the site's two (`ChessBoardLazy`, `EloTable`), and platform pieces that take their data and their actions as props: **every piece of `components/platform/shared/` but `ImageUpload`** (which imports a server action and waits for the module mocks below), and the pieces of the sections that a screen is built from — the shell's menu and search, the course and class cards, the staff's panels and tabs, the explorer's lists, the lesson's outline and skeleton, the dashboard's statistics — plus a few forms and lists mounted with a stub in place of the server action. Where a state only exists after an interaction (a list open, a form filled in or submitted invalid) the story has a `play` function that performs it, so the built Storybook shows that state without a click; `grep -rlw "play" --include="*.stories.tsx" components` names the files that have one. The walkthrough below is what was done for `PlanCard`, written so the next component follows the same path. What the convention test holds, for every stories file: its `title` is its path as the first rule of step 2 says, with the export of a component of its folder — a piece or a section — as the leaf; the page of that component has the «Story» row right after «Stylesheet», naming the file; and every story is an `export const` with a `/** … */` line above it. The [overview](#overview), the foundations, the primitives and the icon gallery are pages: their title names the page, and they have no component page to be named on. For `components/platform/shared/` it also holds that every piece lives in a folder of its own with its stories file beside it, with `ImageUpload` the one listed exception, and why. Whether every state that matters has its story is a matter of review.

## Overview

**platform › Overview**, the first entry of the sidebar (`options.storySort` in `.storybook/preview.tsx`), is the sheet a UI kit opens with: every piece of the platform at a glance, in tiles grouped as Foundations, Icons, Layout, Actions, Forms, Feedback, Data, Cards, Navigation, Overlays, Play, Chess and Adventure, each tile with a link to the Docs page of its own story. It lives in `components/platform/overview/` and, like the foundations, is a page and not a component of the app; unlike them it has no Docs page (`tags: ["!autodocs"]`), which would only draw the sheet a second time inside a frame, so Storybook opens on its canvas.

- **What a tile draws.** The real piece with sample data, mirroring the first story of its file: the primitives as their classes, the shared pieces and the pieces of the sections as their components, a section's dialog form inside its real `<dialog>` behind the button its screen shows, the move menu behind a trigger (mounted open it takes the focus and the sheet would scroll to it). A change to a piece shows on the sheet with nothing touched; what is written by hand is which pieces it shows and their sample data — invented people and public historic games (Morphy's «Opera game»), never a fixture with real players. The foundations' tiles read the same modules as their pages (the contrast of «Text on a surface» is measured on `platform.css` in the theme on screen; «Large screens» sets the values of the 1536px block as custom properties). `SubmitButton`'s pending state is left to its own story: an action that never settles would hold every transition of the sheet.
- **One address for the whole sheet.** The story sets `nextjs.navigation.pathname` to a staff member on a course's syllabus (`staffRoutes.courseSyllabus`), so the pieces that light what matches the address — `PlatformNav` with the staff menu, `StaffTabs`, `OutlineList` — each show their current item.
- **Layout.** The sheet is a design file's canvas: under the page's header, `.overview__canvas` takes the window's height and scrolls both ways (it is wider than the window on purpose), on the sunken grey with a grid of dots that moves with what is on it. Each section is a group with its name on a tab, 1840 px wide, its frames laid in rows across it. Each tile is a **frame of a fixed size** — `width` and `height`, both required, in px — with its name, its size and its Docs link above it, as a design file writes them; the frame is square, filled with the page's background and outlined by a hairline, and the piece is drawn in it with its real styles. The sizes were measured: every frame is as tall as what it shows at its width, rounded up to 8 px with 8 more to spare, and a new tile is given its size the same way. A frame is a scroller (`overflow: auto`) that never scrolls: a piece that keeps something in view (a list of moves) moves its nearest scroller, which is then the frame and has nowhere to go, instead of the canvas. A piece that in the app lives inside a white panel or bar, and writes its quieter text in `--platform-text-subtle` — the grey made for white, under 4.5:1 on the page's background — is given that white `surface` (the menu, the search, the staff's tabs, the level scale, the engine panel, the game review and its tools); the captions on the canvas are in `--platform-text-muted`.
- **It stays complete.** `components/stories.test.ts` requires a tile for every story titled `platform/…`, `chess/…`, `icons/…` or `adventure/…` (the site's are not the platform's pieces), reading the tiles' `docs="…"` ids, and fails on a tile whose id no story has. The one story without a tile is `CurrentPathField`, which draws nothing; it is listed in the test with that reason. A new story therefore comes with its tile (`AGENTS.md`, «Componentes»).

## Foundations

Before the components, eight pages draw what every component of the platform is made of. They live in `components/platform/foundations/` with titles `platform/foundations/<Name>`, and none of them is a component of the app: each is a story that renders a page.

| Page                  | What it shows                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                | Where it reads it from                                                                                                     |
| --------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Colours**           | Every `--platform-*` colour as a swatch painted with its token, by family (the families of the token table in [Styling](/ui/styling#tokens)), with its light and dark values — the one on screen in ink — and the comment written above it; then the two shadows.                            | The `:root` of `app/(platform)/platform.css`, read by `lib/platform-tokens.ts`                                             |
| **Text on a surface** | Every allowed pair of text and background, as a line of real text on its fill with its contrast in the theme on screen and in the other one; then the subtle grey on each fill, the pair the rule forbids, seen failing.                                                                     | `TEXT_PAIRS` of `lib/platform-tokens.ts`, measured on the values of `platform.css`                                         |
| **Corners**           | The three radii and the exception, each on the shape it is for, with how many times the stylesheets write it.                                                                                                                                                                                | The platform's stylesheets, counted                                                                                        |
| **Motion**            | A page's entrance, a dialog, a popup and the accent button's press, each played by the class or component that owns it, with a way to play it again, and the rule it follows where it has one.                                                                                               | The browser: what `getComputedStyle` reports for each element                                                              |
| **Type**              | The font tokens, each drawn in its font with the weights its first font has a face for; every `font-size` and `font-weight` the stylesheets write, with how many times, and the fonts a stylesheet names instead of reading a token. Since the type scale, the sizes written are its tokens. | The `:root` of `platform.css` for the fonts, the browser for their faces, the platform's stylesheets for the rest, counted |
| **Type scale**        | The platform's ten font sizes, the `--platform-type-*` tokens, each drawn at its size with how many times the stylesheets write it, and what is still written off the scale (the board's coordinates).                                                                                       | The `:root` of `platform.css` for the steps; the platform's stylesheets for the uses, counted                              |
| **Spacing scale**     | The twelve spaces on a 4px grid, the `--platform-space-*` tokens, each as a bar of its width with how many times it is written, and the spaces still in px (hairlines, the geometry inside a `calc()`).                                                                                      | The same                                                                                                                   |
| **Large screens**     | What each type and space token takes from 1536px wide (Tailwind's `2xl`): every step at both sizes, and a piece of a screen made of real components drawn at the scale below 1536px and at the one above, side by side (the right column sets the larger values as custom properties).       | The `@media (width >= 1536px)` block of `platform.css`                                                                     |

**The values on them are not copied by hand.** `platform-stylesheets.const.ts` imports the platform's stylesheets as text (`import.meta.glob` with `?raw`, over the same folders the convention tests read, this folder's own stylesheet left out of the counts), and `lib/platform-tokens.ts` reads the tokens out of `platform.css` — the same module, and so the same reading, as `components/platform/platform-tokens.test.ts`; the faces of each font come from the browser (`document.fonts`), and the motion from `getComputedStyle`. Editing a value in a stylesheet reloads the page with it, and a token added to the `:root` appears on Colours with no story touched. What is written by hand, on purpose:

- a **family** of colours: a token whose name no family of `COLOUR_GROUPS` (in `lib/platform-tokens.ts`) recognises is drawn under "Other" until one does, and the convention test fails if two families claim the same token;
- the **pairs** of text and background, `TEXT_PAIRS`: the test measures every pair in it in both themes, measures every rule that sets both its own colour and its own fill, and fails on such a rule whose pair is not listed, so the page shows every pair a rule writes; a text that inherits its colour onto a fill set by another rule cannot be read from the stylesheets and is measured only if its pair is listed, so a new pair of that kind goes there by review;
- the **radii** and their "when", quoted from [Corners in the platform](/ui/styling#corners-in-the-platform); the rule itself is `border-radius.test.ts`;
- the **rules** of the Motion page (160 ms in and 120 ms out for a popup; the dialog's 160 and 200 ms), written as `AGENTS.md` writes them, above what the browser reports, so a stylesheet that drifts from its rule shows the difference on the page; and the prose of every page, which describes and does not measure.

The pages are drawn with the platform's own primitives (`.platform-page`, `.platform-card`, the buttons) and a stylesheet of their own, `foundations.css`, which keeps the platform's rules like any other: corners of 8px, no font named, every text on a fill it reads on. Text on a surface tells the Accessibility panel to skip its counter-examples (`parameters.a11y.context.exclude`): they are under 4.5:1 on purpose.

## Primitives

The global classes of `platform.css` that every screen writes straight into its markup have a story each, in `components/platform/primitives/` with titles `platform/primitives/<Name>`: **Button** (quiet, accent, ink, danger, compact, side by side, disabled, focused), **RowAction** (default, danger, disabled, in a list, focused), **Card** (with and without its title, several on the canvas), **Tag** (neutral and accent) and **Page** (its three widths, inside the shell's `<main>` from `platform-shell.section.css`, which is what caps a page that does not cap itself; each prints the width it is drawn at). They are plain markup with the class — no component exists for them; the frame `.platform-dialog` has none of its own, since every popup is [`PlatformPopup`](/components/platform/shared/platform-popup) or [`PlatformConfirmPopup`](/components/platform/shared/platform-confirm-popup), whose stories open it — and where [Global helpers](/ui/styling#global-helpers), the cross-cutting rules of the same page or the stylesheet's comment say when a variant is used, the story's JSDoc quotes that sentence rather than rewriting it; the few states nothing documents (a disabled row action, a card without its title, the two tags' colours) say what the CSS draws. `.platform-page_height_screen` has no story: it is the lesson's and needs the whole shell. The controls flatten the modifiers into a variant, a size and a state, so a combination nobody wrote a story for can still be looked at.

Two of their states only show with the keyboard or the pointer. A **focused** story tabs to the control in its `play` function, and the ring is drawn, also when the story is reached from the sidebar (checked in the Storybook UI); a screenshot from headless Chrome shows it only with `Emulation.setFocusEmulationEnabled` on, because a page without the window's focus never paints `:focus-visible`. A **hover** has no story, since no script can produce one: hover the control.

## Adding a component: the PlanCard walkthrough

### 1. Give the component its own folder

A component with a story has three files, so it gets a folder named after it, inside its section:

```text
components/site/sections/home/plans/
  plans.section.tsx
  plans-mobile.comp.tsx
  plan-card/
    plan-card.comp.tsx
    plan-card.comp.css
    plan-card.stories.tsx
```

Move with `git mv` so history follows, then fix the importers (`./plan-card.comp` → `./plan-card/plan-card.comp` in `plans.section.tsx` and `plans-mobile.comp.tsx`) and the `File` row of the component's page under `docs/content/components/`. File names keep the usual suffixes; the story adds `.stories.tsx`.

### 2. Write the story file (CSF3)

```tsx
import type { Meta, StoryObj } from "@storybook/nextjs-vite";
import type { PackagePlanContent } from "@/services/home/home.types";
import { PlanCard } from "./plan-card.comp";

const meta = {
  title: "site/sections/home/plans/PlanCard",
  args: defaults,
  argTypes: {/* one entry per control, see step 3 */},
  render: (args) => <PlanCard plan={toPlan(args)} />,
  decorators: [/* see step 4 */],
} satisfies Meta<PlanArgs>;

export default meta;
type Story = StoryObj<typeof meta>;

/** The regular plan as the CMS ships it: no discount, dark button. */
export const Default: Story = {};

/** The highlighted plan: accent border and background, orange button. */
export const Featured: Story = {
  args: { name: "Trimestral", price: 3200, period: "trimestre", featured: true },
};

/** A plan with a previous price: the struck-through row appears under the price. */
export const WithDiscount: Story = { args: { previousPrice: 1500 } };
```

Rules that came out of it:

- **`title` mirrors the path** with the product first and the export name as the leaf, in place of the component's own folder: `site/sections/home/plans/PlanCard` for `components/site/sections/home/plans/plan-card/`. A story that lives in a folder shared by several components keeps that folder: `platform/sections/staff/courses/course-sources/CourseSourceForm`. The sidebar of Storybook then matches `components/` and the docs section.
- **One story per meaningful state**, not one per prop: the default, the highlighted card, the card with a discount. Each story overrides only what differs from `meta.args`.
- **A JSDoc line above each story.** Autodocs prints it under the story's heading.
- Story identifiers are English; the rendered copy stays Spanish, like the app.
- Data is typed with the domain type from `services/*/*.types.ts` (`PackagePlanContent`), never an untyped literal.

### 3. Flatten the props into controls

`PlanCard` takes a single `plan` object. Left as is, the controls panel shows one JSON editor, which is useless for flipping states. The story declares a flat args type instead, one field per control, and `render` folds them back into the prop:

```tsx
type PlanArgs = Omit<PackagePlanContent, "features"> & { features: string };

render: ({ features, ...rest }) => (
  <PlanCard plan={{ ...rest, features: features.split("\n").filter(Boolean) }} />
),
```

`features` is edited as text with one line per item and split in `render`. Each arg then gets an `argTypes` entry with the right control, a description and a category:

```tsx
argTypes: {
  name: { control: "text", description: "Plan name (title of the card).", table: { category: "Copy" } },
  price: { control: { type: "number", min: 0, step: 50 }, table: { category: "Price" } },
  previousPrice: {
    control: { type: "number", min: 0, step: 50 },
    description: "When set, shown struck through under the price.",
    // Its default is `undefined`, which autodocs would otherwise report as `object`.
    table: { category: "Price", type: { summary: "number" } },
  },
  currency: { control: "select", options: ["MXN", "USD", "EUR"], table: { category: "Price" } },
  featured: { control: "boolean", table: { category: "State" } },
},
```

Two things to know:

- **Do not set `component`** in `meta` when the args are not the component's props. With `component: PlanCard`, Storybook merges `PlanCardProps` into the args type and every story fails typecheck asking for `plan`. Autodocs still builds the table from `argTypes`.
- **`table.type.summary`** fixes the type autodocs infers from a default value: an `undefined` default shows as `object`, an array edited as text shows as `string`.

### 4. Reproduce the component's real context with a decorator

The first screenshot of the non-featured card was unreadable: dark text on the dark site background. In the app the card always sits inside `PlansSection`, whose `.section_theme_light` sets the text colour, and the card inherits it. The story wraps itself in that section:

```tsx
decorators: [
  // In the app the card sits inside `.section_theme_light` (plans.section.tsx)
  // and inherits its colour; without it the non-featured card is unreadable.
  (Story) => (
    <section className="section section_theme_light" style={{ padding: 32 }}>
      <div style={{ maxWidth: 360 }}>
        <Story />
      </div>
    </section>
  ),
],
```

This is the kind of coupling Storybook makes visible. When you find one, wrap the story in the same global class the parent renders (`.section`, `.section_theme_*`, `.platform-theme`…) and say why in a comment; do not change the component to make the story work.

### 5. Document the component in Storybook

Autodocs builds a Docs page per story file. It fills up from three places:

- **`parameters.docs.description.component`** in `meta`: a Markdown string with what the component is, who renders it, its states and a link back to its page in this site (`/components/site/sections/home/plans/plan-card`).
- **JSDoc on each story** (step 2).
- **`description` on each `argTypes` entry** (step 3).

Also add JSDoc to the component itself, on the props interface and on the function, in English like every comment:

```tsx
interface PlanCardProps {
  /** One plan of the `home-packages` Global, already mapped by services/home. */
  plan: PackagePlanContent;
  /** Extra class on the block; the mobile wrapper passes `plans-mobile__card`. */
  className?: string;
}
```

### 6. Update the reference page

The component's page under `docs/content/components/` gets a `Story` row in its facts table, after `Stylesheet`, with the story file and its title:

```md
| Stylesheet | `plan-card.comp.css` → block `.plan-card` |
| Story | `plan-card/plan-card.stories.tsx` (`npm run storybook` → site/sections/home/plans/PlanCard: Default, Featured, WithDiscount) |
```

### 7. Check

```bash
npm run format:check && npm run lint && npm run typecheck && npm test
npm run storybook:build
npm run test:storybook   # the story as a test, under axe, in light and in dark
npm run storybook        # open the story, flip the two toolbars, open the Docs tab
```

A story is done when the build and `npm run test:storybook` are green, every state renders as it does in the app under both toolbar themes and in light and dark where it applies, and the Docs tab shows the description, the controls table and one section per story. Look at the Accessibility panel in light and in dark before calling it done: axe only sees what is on screen, so a menu, a list or a dialog has to be open — which is what a `play` function is for — and a violation it reports under one theme (contrast, mostly) may not exist under the other. The panel is where you look; the gate is `npm run test:storybook`, which runs the same axe on every story in light and in dark and fails the one with a violation ([Stories as tests](#stories-as-tests)).

## Stories as tests

`npm run test:storybook` (`vitest run --config vitest.storybook.config.mts`) makes every story a test, through the Vitest plugin of `@storybook/addon-vitest`, in Vitest's browser mode: a headless Chromium driven by Playwright. Each story is rendered as the static build renders it — the preview with its decorators, both stylesheets and the fonts, `public/` at its URLs —, its `play` function runs, and then addon-a11y runs axe on what is left on screen. Every story runs twice, in the projects `storybook-light` and `storybook-dark`, which set the `scheme` toolbar global to each theme (the plugin's `initialGlobals`): a contrast can fail in one theme and pass in the other. The theme toolbar stays at `auto`.

- **What fails a story**: an error while it renders, an `expect` or a query of its `play` that fails, and any axe violation, because `.storybook/preview.tsx` sets `parameters.a11y.test` to `"error"`. The rules are axe's defaults (WCAG 2.x A and AA, plus its best practices) but `region`, which the addon turns off because a story has no landmarks. What axe can only leave for review (`incomplete`) passes: a text of one character is never measured for contrast, for instance, so `StudyStreak`'s single-digit figures go unchecked.
- **What axe sees** is what the story leaves on screen once its `play` is done: a menu, a list, a tooltip or a dialog is only checked if a `play` opens it. In this runner Storybook does not wait for animations: before axe it pauses every CSS animation on its last frame and turns transitions off. A popup still leaving would never finish, and Radix keeps the rest of the page `aria-hidden` until its exit animation ends, so a `play` that closes something waits until it is gone (`await waitFor(() => expect(canvas.queryByRole("listbox")).toBeNull())`, as `CourseSourceForm`'s `DigitalCourse` does); and one that checks a popup it has just opened asks for it to be there (`toBeInTheDocument`), not `toBeVisible`, which fails on the first frame of a fade.
- **A known failure** is marked on the story that has it (on the meta when every story of the file has it), with a comment that says what fails and names the rule, and it stays until the component is fixed:

  ```tsx
  /** The calendar open on the chosen day's month. */
  export const Open: Story = {
    // a11y: todo until the popover has an accessible name (aria-dialog-name).
    parameters: { a11y: { test: "todo" } },
    play: async ({ canvasElement }) => {
      await userEvent.click(within(canvasElement).getByRole("button", { name: "Fecha de la partida" }));
    },
  };
  ```

  axe still runs on it and reports the violation as a warning, in the run and in the Accessibility panel, but the test passes; `grep -rn "a11y: todo" components` lists them. None is marked today: the three stories axe failed when the runner arrived (`PlatformDatePicker`'s and `PlatformSelect`'s `Open`, `StudyStreak`'s long streak) were fixed before it landed. A story whose violation is a false positive keeps the rule and leaves out only the element, with the reason beside it: `PlatformSelect`'s `Open` scopes `aria-hidden-focus` off its own trigger (`a11y.config.rules`), which Radix hides with the rest of the page while the list holds the focus. A rule is never switched off for every story (`a11y.config.rules` in the preview) without a comment that says why, and a deliberate counter-example is left out by its element, not by its rule: Text on a surface excludes its own (`a11y.context.exclude`).

- **In CI** the last three steps of `.github/workflows/ci.yml` are these: `npm run storybook:build`, so a story that breaks the static build fails the job, then `npx playwright install --with-deps --only-shell chromium` and `npm run test:storybook` ([Testing & CI](/operations/testing-and-ci#continuous-integration)).
- **Locally**, Playwright needs its Chromium once: `npx playwright install chromium`. The two projects take some 40 s on a laptop; a folder or a file runs alone with `npm run test:storybook -- components/platform/shared/platform-select`.
- **Not set up**: the addon's test widget in Storybook's sidebar, which runs the same tests from the UI. It looks for the stories' project in `vitest.config.*`, and that file is `npm test`'s alone on purpose: Vitest builds every project a config lists before `--project` filters them, and building the stories' loads Storybook, Next's config and Next's `.env` files into the process — some 2.5 s more on every `npm test`, and the local `.env` files in a run that CI makes without them.

## What Storybook does not cover yet

- **Sections that read services** (`export async function` calling `services/*/*.service.ts`) and **client components that import a server action** cannot run in the browser as they are; a form that has a story is the piece that receives the action as a prop (`NewStudyForm`, and not `NewStudy`, which imports `createStudy`); `ImageUpload`, whose hook `useImageUpload` imports `createUploadSignature`, is the shared piece left without a story for that reason, and for the same one the sheet has no tile yet for `PlatformUserMenu`, `StudyCard`, the hosts of a confirmation (`DeleteStudy`, `ResetProgress`, `RemoveCourse`: the confirmation itself is on the sheet as `PlatformConfirmPopup`, and only the host that binds the action is not), `CourseStatusMenu`, `LearnMode`, `LessonOutline` and the authors' manager and form. The plan for them is a manual mock per module in `services/<domain>/__mocks__/` registered with `sb.mock()` in `preview.tsx`, plus typed fixtures in `lib/testing/fixtures/`. Not started.
- **Chess components** have their stories (`chess/…`), served from `public/engine`, `public/sounds` and `public/pieces` through `staticDirs`. Three things are out of reach: the promotion dialog (chessground ignores the events a script sends, so `ChessBoard`'s `Interactive` story leaves the drag to the hand), full screen (it needs a user gesture) and `GameReview`'s failed state (only a worker that fails reaches it). `GameReview`'s `Running` story runs the real engine, so what it shows depends on how fast the engine is. A `play` that types into a React-controlled `<textarea>` with `userEvent.type` can reach the DOM and not the component's `onChange` (seen on the comment of `AnalysisBoard`, an editor since removed: the input events arrive, React's value stays): such a story sets the value with `fireEvent.input(field, { target: { value } })`, which writes it the way React reads it, and checks the result. In Storybook the standalone board never shows the site's dark colours: its stylesheet counts on the `--platform-*` tokens being undefined outside the platform, and the preview always loads `platform.css`.
- A dev-only console error in the docs site ("Encountered a script tag…") is unrelated: it comes from `next-themes` inside Nextra, see `IMPROVEMENTS.md` #71.
